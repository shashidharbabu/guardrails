========================================================================
  WHY THE RAG FAILED & HOW WE FIXED THE CHUNKS
  Project: Enterprise Guardrails RAG — Regulatory Documents
  Date: 2026-03-26
========================================================================

PROBLEM
-------
The previous RAG pipeline was returning poor chunks during retrieval.
Multi-agent debate system needs precise, self-contained regulatory
excerpts — but the chunks it was getting were broken, noisy, or
too vague to reason over.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
DIAGNOSIS: What was wrong with the old chunks (4,664 chunks, 39 docs)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. BOUNDARY BREAKS — the #1 problem
   - 41.4% of chunks ended mid-sentence
   - 18.5% started mid-sentence
   - Example: chunk ends with "...roles, responsibilities, and authorities to"
     and the next chunk continues "foster accountability, performance assessment..."
   - Impact: retrieval returns half-thoughts that agents can't reason over

2. SIZE PROBLEMS
   - 169 chunks under 100 tokens (too small — just headers or fragments)
   - 215 chunks over 500 tokens (too large — diluted relevance)
   - Shortest chunk: 20 tokens — just "IV. MODEL DEVELOPMENT > Model Use"
   - Longest chunk: 794 tokens — a pure article cross-reference table

3. JUNK CONTENT
   - 12 table-of-contents chunks (dotted lines + page numbers)
   - 6 chunks with fewer than 20 meaningful words
   - 1 header-only chunk with no actual content

4. NEAR-DUPLICATES
   - 122 chunk pairs with >85% text overlap (mostly in NIST SSDF, CCPA)
   - Caused by paragraph-grouped chunking repeating context

5. CONTEXT CONTINUITY
   - 21.8% of consecutive chunk pairs had broken splits
   - Meaning was cut across boundaries with no overlap

------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIXES: What we changed (5,903 chunks after rechunking)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

FIX 1: Sentence-aware splitting (spaCy)
   - Replaced naive paragraph-grouped chunking with spaCy sentence
     boundary detection
   - Chunks now always start and end at sentence boundaries
   - Result: bad-end boundaries 41.4% -> 7.3%
   - Result: bad-start boundaries 18.5% -> 3.3%

FIX 2: Structure-aware heading detection
   - MAJOR headings (Article, Section, Chapter) always force new chunk
   - MINOR headings ((a), (1), 4.1) only break if chunk is already
     >= 150 tokens — prevents tiny fragments from list items
   - Regex patterns for regulatory structure:
     Article X, Section X, Chapter X, Clause X, Recital X

FIX 3: Token limits tuned for regulatory text
   - Target: 300-480 tokens (was 300-400, too tight)
   - Max: 512 tokens (was 450 — long legal sentences need room)
   - Min: 150 tokens
   - Drop anything under 50 tokens
   - Result: 48.3% of chunks in 300-400 sweet spot (was 43.7%)

FIX 4: Oversized sentence splitting at punctuation
   - Sentences exceeding 512 tokens (rare, mostly enumeration lists)
     split at nearest comma or semicolon — not mid-word
   - 53 chunks needed this treatment
   - Marked with split_at_punctuation: true in metadata

FIX 5: 50-token overlap between consecutive chunks
   - Each chunk starts with the last 50 tokens of the previous chunk
   - Preserves context continuity for retrieval
   - 99.3% of chunks have overlap

FIX 6: Junk elimination
   - TOC lines (dotted patterns): 12 -> 0
   - Header-only chunks: dropped
   - Pure cross-reference tables: dropped
   - Under-100-token chunks: 169 -> 3

FIX 7: Metadata enrichment
   - article_number extracted and tagged (41.3% of chunks)
   - starts_at_boundary flag for structural awareness
   - tier (T0/T1/T2/T3) preserved from source
   - doc_name, source_url carried through

------------------------------------------------------------------------
KEY LEARNINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Regulatory text is NOT like normal prose — sentences routinely end
   with : ; , (enumeration headers, definitions). These are valid
   boundaries, not broken ones. Had to expand "good ending" character
   set beyond just . ? !

2. Naive paragraph chunking destroys legal meaning. A single GDPR
   article can span 3 pages — splitting by paragraph count ignores
   sentence structure entirely.

3. List items like (a) (b) (c) look like headings but forcing a break
   at every one creates 50-token fragments. Major vs minor heading
   distinction was critical.

4. Overlap matters more for legal text than for general content.
   Legal clauses reference "the foregoing" and "as mentioned above" —
   without overlap, these references point to nothing.

5. Token count distribution matters more than averages. The old chunks
   had acceptable mean (334) but terrible tails (20-794 range).
   The new chunks have a tighter distribution (54-563, std 81 vs 114).

========================================================================
  PART 2: CONTEXTUAL ENRICHMENT — THE MINIMAX BATTLE
  Date: 2026-03-27
========================================================================

WHAT WE DID
------------
Implemented Anthropic's "Contextual Retrieval" technique:
prepend each chunk with 1-2 sentences of document-aware context
generated by an LLM (MiniMax M2.7 via OpenRouter), so the embedding
captures regulation name, article/section, and concept — not just
raw legal text.

Example before:
  "The controller shall ensure compliance with paragraph 1 and
   implement appropriate technical measures..."

Example after:
  "This excerpt covers Article 25 of the EU GDPR, addressing the
   obligation of data protection by design and by default.
   The controller shall ensure compliance with paragraph 1 and
   implement appropriate technical measures..."

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR REGULATORY RAG
------------------------------------
39 different regulations use near-identical language:
  - "data protection" appears in GDPR, PIPL, PDPA, APPI, PDPL...
  - "consent" appears in every privacy law
  - "risk assessment" appears in ISO 27001, NIST CSF, AI Act...

Without context, embeddings can't distinguish which law a chunk
comes from. The context prefix disambiguates at embedding time,
not retrieval time — 49% fewer retrieval failures per Anthropic's
research, likely higher for regulatory text.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DEBUGGING JOURNEY (interview-worthy)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

ATTEMPT 1: Basic API call
  - max_tokens: 150, temperature: 0.3
  - Result: ~85% success, 15% returning None
  - Diagnosis: didn't know why

ATTEMPT 2: Prompt in system message only
  - Moved all instructions to system prompt, bare user message
  - Result: 100% NULL responses
  - Root cause: MiniMax M2.7 needs task instructions in the USER
    message, not system. The system message sets persona, the user
    message must contain the actual task. Model-specific behavior.
  - Lesson: don't assume prompt patterns transfer across models

ATTEMPT 3: Prompt restored, still some nulls
  - Same prompt that worked before, still ~10-15% null
  - Added reasoning chain extraction as fallback
  - Result: got responses, but they were garbage:
    "2 (10) covering (11) the (12) planning (13) phase (14) of..."
  - Root cause: MiniMax M2.7 is a REASONING MODEL. Every response
    has two parts:
      reasoning: ~80-100 tokens (chain-of-thought, internal)
      content:   ~50-80 tokens (actual answer)
    With max_tokens: 150-200, some chunks used all tokens on
    reasoning, leaving nothing for content → content = null

THE FIX THAT WORKED
  - max_tokens: 150 → 500 (budget for reasoning + content)
  - Only accept the content field (reasoning is noisy garbage)
  - If content is null, DON'T extract from reasoning — retry instead
  - Three-pass strategy for 100% coverage

FINAL ARCHITECTURE: THREE-PASS ENRICHMENT
------------------------------------------
Pass 1 — Batch enrichment
  - 5 chunks per batch, 2s delay between batches
  - 3 retries per chunk with 20-40s timeout
  - Progress saved every 200 chunks (resumable on crash)
  - Result: 5,899/5,903 enriched (99.93%)

Pass 2 — Repair mode
  - Failed chunks get 5 more attempts
  - Exponential backoff: 3s, 6s, 12s, 24s, 48s
  - Longer timeouts: 30s, 40s, 50s
  - Result: repaired 4/4 on first retry

Pass 3 — Smart metadata fallback (never needed)
  - Doc name map: 37 regulations → human-readable names
  - Topic detection from chunk text (26 regex patterns)
  - Article/section extraction from content
  - Would produce: "This excerpt is from EU GDPR, Article 35.
    This section addresses impact assessment requirements."

FINAL RESULT: 5,903/5,903 — 100% LLM-generated context

COST & TIME
  - Total cost: ~$2.40 (MiniMax M2.7 via OpenRouter)
  - Total time: ~13 hours (batch size 5, 2s delay)
  - All traced in LangSmith (project: guardrails-rag)
  - Every call has input/output/latency visible

OBSERVABILITY (LangSmith)
  - @traceable on enrich_single_chunk (run_type="llm")
  - @traceable on process_batch (run_type="chain")
  - Batch → individual call nesting in waterfall view
  - Caught the null content issue by inspecting traces

------------------------------------------------------------------------
KEY LEARNINGS (for interviews)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. REASONING MODELS NEED TOKEN BUDGET FOR THINKING
   MiniMax M2.7, DeepSeek, o1 — reasoning models use tokens
   internally before producing output. If max_tokens is too low,
   the model "thinks" but never "answers." Always set max_tokens
   2-3x higher than expected output for reasoning models.

2. PROMPT PLACEMENT MATTERS PER MODEL
   GPT/Claude: system message instructions work fine
   MiniMax M2.7: task MUST be in user message, system is just persona
   Always test prompt placement when switching models.

3. NULL DOESN'T MEAN FAILURE — IT MEANS INVESTIGATE
   Our first instinct was "API is broken." Reality was: the API
   worked perfectly, we just weren't giving it enough token budget.
   The LangSmith traces proved this — we could see reasoning tokens
   being generated but content staying null.

4. RESUMABILITY IS NON-NEGOTIABLE FOR LONG JOBS
   5,903 API calls over 13 hours. Without checkpoint saving every
   200 chunks, any crash = full restart = double the cost.
   Progress file + purge-bad-on-resume pattern saved us multiple
   times during debugging iterations.

5. THREE-PASS > SINGLE-PASS FOR 100% COVERAGE
   Pass 1 catches 99.9%. Pass 2 catches the rest with patience.
   Pass 3 exists as insurance. This pattern works for any bulk
   API enrichment pipeline.

6. CONTEXTUAL RETRIEVAL IS HIGH-ROI FOR DOMAIN-SPECIFIC RAG
   Anthropic's 49% failure reduction was on general documents.
   For regulatory text where 39 laws use identical vocabulary,
   disambiguation via context prefix is arguably more important
   than the embedding model choice itself.

========================================================================
  THREE-STAGE CHUNK COMPARISON
========================================================================

Stage 1: Original chunks (from previous pipeline)
  - 4,664 chunks, naive paragraph splitting
  - 41.4% broken boundaries, junk/TOC noise
  - No metadata, no overlap, no structure awareness

Stage 2: Rechunked (spaCy + structure-aware)
  - 5,903 chunks, sentence-boundary splitting
  - 7.3% broken boundaries, zero junk
  - 50-token overlap, article metadata, tier tagging
  - Token distribution: tight 200-400 range

Stage 3: Enriched (+ MiniMax M2.7 context)
  - 5,903 chunks, each with LLM-generated context prefix
  - 100% coverage, zero fallbacks
  - enriched_text = context + chunk (ready for embedding)
  - Embedding will capture regulation + article + concept

Each stage builds on the previous. Skip any stage and the
retrieval quality drops significantly. The pipeline is:

  PDF → old chunks → rechunk → enrich → embed → Qdrant
                        ↑          ↑
                    you are here   done
